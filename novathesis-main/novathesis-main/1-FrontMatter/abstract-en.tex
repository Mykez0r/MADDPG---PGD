Routing controllers trained with Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient
(MADDPG), often combined with graph neural networks, decide where to send traffic from
the telemetry each agent perceives, and that telemetry is something an attacker may be
able to corrupt. This dissertation asks whether the apparent robustness such a
controller showed against the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) survives an attacker that
is stronger in principle. It cannot be taken for granted: the earlier attack perturbed
each agent in isolation and at every time step, so its limited effect on packet delivery
cannot distinguish a genuinely robust controller from an attack too weak to expose it.
The attack built here is the learned observation adversary of the State-Adversarial
Markov Decision Process, extended with two mechanisms, namely perturbations chosen
jointly for all compromised agents, as SAJA suggests, and divided across time under a
budget spent at the moments when the network's redundancy is thinnest, as PGD and
strategically-timed attacks suggest. Twenty-eight adversaries, one for each combination
of victim variant, attack scope and domain constraint, were trained against seven frozen
MADDPG variants and evaluated on 30 paired episodes against an unperturbed arm and a
random control drawn from the same admissible set, at four timing budgets and without
one, and were compared with FGSM on the 15 episodes the two studies share. The learned
adversary did more damage than the baseline in only one of the 70 comparable
configurations, and coordination and the timing budget each changed which victims
were harmed. The effects measured are small, however, mostly of the same order as the
movement that a perturbation of this size produces with no adversarial direction at all,
and each configuration was trained once, so the differences between configurations
cannot be separated from the variation between training runs. The evidence therefore
supports the weaker claim that no attack in this family did substantial damage to these
controllers, rather than establishing that they are robust, and it leaves open how much
of the variation observed is attributable to the adversary rather than to noise.
